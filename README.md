# inhaca_island_mangroves
Mapping mangroves on inhaca island for geos 472/ MSc thesis
Mangrove decline in Maputo Bay Mozambique
Who is the map for?
Well the map is selfishly for myself and my masters thesis. My research area focuses on Southern Mozambique on Inhaca island, an extremely rich marine resource area. This map will help myself and my collogues in Mozambique/ my professor to access areas in Mozambique that have declining mangrove estuaries. There are papers that discuss the decline of mangroves in Mozambique but it is helpful to visualize the changes through the years as well to predict the losses that could occur in the future. Surprisingly around Inhaca island we have seen an increase, but closer to Maputo river we see a decline, hypothesizing that areas that collect sediment would lead to a decline in mangrove estuaries.
Between 1996 and 2020, the calculated net change in mangrove coverage was approximately:
•	Maputo Bay overall: 1,143 ha decline (~13%)
•	Inhaca Island and Machangulo combined: 202 ha decline (~15%)
•	Mainland: 942 ha decline (~13%)
Styling 
I deleted around 100 basemap layers from the dataset I downloaded. There were basemap layers that did not a significant impact to the overall design of the map. The goal was to show the change in mangroves across time, therefore if I looked at road or ferry access across the island it would effect the overall hypothesis. 
Styling the mangroves I wanted the mangroves to stand out against the satellite, this is why I chose a bright purple to contrast against the green. This creates a clear visual hierarchy, allowing the mangroves to stand out immediately. 
As users zoom in, the map becomes increasingly focused on the mangrove areas, allowing them to examine the spatial patterns in detail. 
 Typography white labels with dark halos on imagery, water labels blue and letter spacing was simple but affective for this map. Then once you zoom in you are able to solely see the mangroves! Along with the visual styling I made a label file since the basemaps labels were empty dots. The labels I chose were in areas where there was a loss of mangroves, as well as large settlements with the exemption of Inhaca island which’s settlement is around 3000, which fun fact is only 1/3 of UBC yearly graduating class. 
For comparison between the two years, I drew on knowledge from a previous JavaScript mapping project. This allowed me to incorporate an interactive comparison rather than relying on two separate static maps. 

Limitations and improvements: Limitations: I want to map the whole coast of Mozambique, but this would have taken too much data and also time. I am happy how this map turned out and I will be helpful for the future to decided what areas we should travel to or study. Critique from Gil, was to change the buttons to different colors.
